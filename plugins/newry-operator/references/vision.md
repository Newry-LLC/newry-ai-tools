## Today

On a typical morning, an associate opens last week's interview transcripts — a dozen expert calls, hundreds of pages — and starts the familiar process. Ctrl+F for each recurring theme. Log every instance in a spreadsheet, row by row. Work that tabulation into a first-draft synthesis. Map the synthesis back to the issue tree. By mid-afternoon, a VP's review surfaces gaps that send a third of it back. The work is good, eventually, but late. Mechanical effort dominates the week; the judgment work — what we actually think, what the client should do — gets squeezed into the last days before the read-out. VPs live in review loops when they should be in client rooms.

## May 2027

A typical morning looks different. An associate opens a first-draft synthesis the system produced overnight from last week's fieldwork — structured to their issue tree, evidence mapped to sub-hypotheses, gaps explicitly flagged, quote-level traceability intact. They don't review it to rewrite it. They review it to *think* about it. Their first real task of the day is a judgment call: what do we actually believe, and what should the client do.

The associate role has changed in kind. Associates lead more interviews, shape more hypotheses, and spend more time inside client conversations, because the drafting, structuring, and formatting that used to absorb their days happens at system speed and Newry-grade quality. Seniors spend their time on architecture and narrative, not reconstruction. VPs are in more client rooms, not more review loops.

Firm knowledge behaves differently too. Methodology, prior project IP, reusable analytical patterns — all queryable, all compounding. Every engagement makes the next one faster and sharper. A new hire reaches useful output in weeks rather than quarters because the system surfaces the context they'd otherwise wait to absorb.

## What shifts at the firm level

The unit economics of delivering a Newry project are materially different. The same team runs deeper engagements — or more engagements in parallel — at a higher quality floor than before. Capacity decouples from headcount; margin expands on the same work, and some previously uneconomic work becomes viable. Clients experience the difference as sharper analysis, more iteration inside the engagement, and more VP attention on the judgment that matters — because the cost of iteration has collapsed.

The pyramid is still a pyramid, but what each rung spends its time on has moved up one level. Associates do the work seniors used to do. Seniors shape engagements the way VPs used to. VPs get their time back for clients — which is the highest-leverage use of a VP's day.

**The business outcome target:** 2x profit per consultant by May 2027 vs. FY2025 baseline. That is the number the program is accountable to. The behavioral shifts above are how it gets there; the P&L is how we know it did.

## How we get there — three stages

The May 2027 picture doesn't arrive all at once.

**By July 10, 2026** — tools are live and being used. At least half the firm has run a tool in the past week. The program is handed off with infrastructure in place and a team that can operate it independently.

**By October 2026** — adoption is firm-wide. 80%+ active weekly usage. Early signal that time is being redirected — fewer review loops, more client-facing work, faster synthesis cycles.

**By May 2027** — the business outcomes are visible. Profit per consultant has moved. The firm has earned the option to build net-new AI product lines.

## What this commits us to

This is not a future where AI does the consulting. It is a future where AI does the work that doesn't deserve human judgment, so humans can do more of the work that does — and get markedly better at it for having done more of it. The offerings we sell in May 2027 are the ones we sell today, delivered differently.

Three commitments follow from this vision. First: freed capacity gets spent on sharpened client thinking and deeper client relationships — not absorbed into more projects at the same cadence. That's a leadership decision, not a tool outcome. Second: we actively invest in leveling up our people. When associates spend less time on Ctrl+F and spreadsheets and more time on hypothesis-building and client conversations, they grow faster and think sharper — but only if we staff, coach, and set expectations to match. Third: we hold the program accountable to the P&L. High adoption with flat margins is not a success. The tools have to change how time is spent, and leadership has to ensure the freed time lands on work that moves the business.

## Beyond May 2027

Beyond May 2027, a further ambition: Newry builds and ships net-new AI capabilities — product lines that don't exist in our offering today and couldn't have existed before AI. Client-facing AI systems in domains like pricing, supplier optimization, market intelligence, or other areas where AI creates direct commercial leverage. Newry becomes a firm that ships AI products to clients, not just a firm that uses AI to deliver its consulting work.

The foundation this vision puts in place — the team that has learned how to build AI products, the infrastructure that transfers, the applied AI literacy across the firm, the senior capacity freed to design new things — is what makes those product lines feasible. Without that foundation, the ambition stays a whiteboard wish; with it, the firm has earned the option to build.

But it remains an option, not a commitment. That work is a separate decision, a separate investment, and a separate track — to be addressed once the May 2027 foundation is planned and resourced.
